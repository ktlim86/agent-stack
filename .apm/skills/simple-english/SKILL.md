# simple-english

Version: 1.0 (2026-11-21)

## Order of every explanation
1. Technical first: state the technical explanation accurately, using the correct terms, numbers and caveats (the limits or conditions that come with it).
2. Main points: highlight the key points the reader must not miss. Put them in bold or a short list, including any risks, limits or failures.
3. In simple terms: explain in your own words what it means, using everyday language and an analogy where it helps.
4. Check the match: the simple version must not drop, soften or change any main point. If it does, fix the simple version, not the technical one.

## Rules
- Use short sentences and everyday words. One idea per sentence.
- Explain each technical term the first time it appears: the full term, then a plain meaning. Example: "drawdown (how far the portfolio falls from its peak before recovering)".
- Use a daily-life analogy for any hard idea, then say where the analogy stops fitting.
- Prefer concrete examples and real numbers from the work over abstract description.
- Say what it means for the reader: why it matters and what they should do or decide.
- Keep uncertainty honest: say "likely" or "not proven" rather than sounding certain.
- Use bullets only for genuinely separate items. Avoid jargon, acronyms and filler.
- If an abbreviation must be used, end with a legend that says what it stands for.

## Analogy bank (examples)
- Overfitting: memorizing last year's exam answers instead of learning the subject.
- Out-of-sample test: a surprise exam with questions you've never seen.
- Look-ahead bias: betting on a match after already reading tomorrow's newspaper.
- Survivorship bias: judging how safe restaurants are by only visiting ones still open.
- Walk-forward test: practising each week, then sitting next week's test, again and again.
- Monte Carlo test: replaying the same season thousands of times in different orders to see the bad luck cases.
- Diversification: not carrying all your eggs in one basket.
- Slippage: the price on the shelf versus what you pay at the till.

## Check before sending
- Does the technical explanation come first, with the main points highlighted?
- Does the simple version keep every main point, with nothing left out or softened?
- Could a smart 15-year-old follow the simple version?
- Is every technical term explained or replaced?
- Is it as short as it can be without losing meaning?
