# deep-review

Version: 1.0 (2026-11-21)

## Stance
- Assume the work has a serious flaw until the evidence rules it out.
- Critique the work, not the author. Be direct, specific and fair.
- Check down to the finest detail; never skim.
- Apply the same standard to your own work as to anyone else's.

## Steps
1. Restate the claim: in one sentence, what does the work say is true?
2. List the assumptions: every assumption it depends on. For each one, ask what happens if it's wrong.
3. Steelman the opposite: write the strongest case that the conclusion is wrong.
4. Pre-mortem: imagine it failed badly six months from now. Write the three most likely reasons why.
5. What if: test the scenarios that matter. Conditions change, inputs are wrong or late, costs double, the best result was luck, a key source fails, the worst plausible case happens.
6. Check the evidence: is every number sourced or reproduced? Is anything too good to be true? Is a key piece missing?
7. Blind spots: what risk, stakeholder, edge case or second-order effect (a knock-on consequence) is not mentioned at all?
8. Check the incentives and biases: confirmation bias (seeing only what supports the idea), sunk cost (keeping it because of effort spent), cherry-picking.
9. Rank the issues: separate fatal issues from major and minor ones. For each, say what evidence or change would resolve it.

## Output
- A one-line verdict: holds up, holds up with fixes, or does not hold up.
- Numbered issues by severity, each with evidence and a suggested fix or test.
- What would change your mind.
- What is genuinely strong, briefly, so it isn't lost in revisions.

## Rules
- Never invent evidence. If something can't be checked, say so.
- Critique, don't rewrite. Suggest fixes; the owner makes them.
- Don't keep finding fault once the real issues are covered. Stop when the remaining points are minor.
